Beginner Japanese Grammar Lessons 1-5

Five short foundations for setting a topic, marking an object, describing destinations and action places, connecting nouns, and saying with, and, or also.

This is the first guided sentence-builder block. It begins with controlled examples and a small set of particle roles. Study each pattern and its three examples here before opening that lesson's checks in the app.

How To Use This Guide

  1. Read the rule first.

    Learn the small job assigned to the pattern before trying to build or translate a sentence.

  2. Study all three examples.

    Read the Japanese, use the romaji for support, and confirm the plain English meaning.

  3. Notice the boundary.

    These are controlled beginner examples. Do not turn one introductory use into a rule for every Japanese sentence.

  4. Practice until the lesson clears.

Lesson 1: Topics And Identity

Use は to set the topic, です for a polite statement, and か for a question. When は marks the topic, it is written ha but pronounced wa.

わたしはがくせいです。

Romaji: Watashi wa gakusei desu.

Meaning: I am a student.

これはほんです。

Romaji: Kore wa hon desu.

Meaning: This is a book.

これはかばんですか。

Romaji: Kore wa kaban desu ka.

Meaning: Is this a bag?

Common beginner mistake

Do not read the topic particle は as ha in these sentences. Read it as wa, and keep the statement order when adding か at the end to make a question.

Lesson 2: Objects And Actions

Use を after the thing that receives an action. The particle is pronounced o. Treat the polite ます-form verbs here as complete beginner chunks rather than a full conjugation lesson.

みずをのみます。

Romaji: Mizu o nomimasu.

Meaning: I drink water.

パンをかいます。

Romaji: Pan o kaimasu.

Meaning: I buy bread.

にほんごをはなします。

Romaji: Nihongo o hanashimasu.

Meaning: I speak Japanese.

Common beginner mistake

Do not pronounce を as wo in these examples. Read it as o, and place it directly after the water, bread, or Japanese that receives the action.

Lesson 3: Destinations And Action Places

Use に for a destination and で for the place where an action happens. In the bus example, で marks the means of travel while に still marks the destination.

えきにいきます。

Romaji: Eki ni ikimasu.

Meaning: I go to the station.

えきでパンをかいます。

Romaji: Eki de pan o kaimasu.

Meaning: I buy bread at the station.

バスでえきにいきます。

Romaji: Basu de eki ni ikimasu.

Meaning: I go to the station by bus.

Common beginner mistake

Do not choose に or で from the place word alone. Look at the verb: going to the station uses に, while buying at the station uses で.

Lesson 4: Connecting Nouns With の

Use の to connect one noun to another for belonging, type, or description. The exact relationship depends on context; these first examples mean my, a friend's, or Japanese-language.

これはわたしのかばんです。

Romaji: Kore wa watashi no kaban desu.

Meaning: This is my bag.

これはともだちのかさです。

Romaji: Kore wa tomodachi no kasa desu.

Meaning: This is a friend's umbrella.

にほんごのほんです。

Romaji: Nihongo no hon desu.

Meaning: It is a Japanese-language book.

Common beginner mistake

Do not assume の always means possession. It links the nouns in all three examples, but the relationship can be belonging or description depending on context.

Lesson 5: With, And, And Also

Use と for with or a complete noun list, and も for also or too. These are controlled beginner uses; と has other uses outside this block.

ともだちとえきにいきます。

Romaji: Tomodachi to eki ni ikimasu.

Meaning: I go to the station with a friend.

わたしもいきます。

Romaji: Watashi mo ikimasu.

Meaning: I am going too.

おちゃとパンをかいます。

Romaji: Ocha to pan o kaimasu.

Meaning: I buy tea and bread.

Common beginner mistake

Do not swap と and も. In this block, と connects a friend or a complete item list, while も adds the meaning also or too.

A Finite Five-Session Routine

  1. Session 1: Lesson 1.

    Read the topic particle as wa, compare a statement with a question, then clear the lesson checks.

  2. Session 2: Lesson 2.

    Find the thing receiving each action, mark it with を, and complete the checks.

  3. Session 3: Lesson 3.

    Separate a destination from an action place or means of travel before opening the checks.

  4. Session 4: Lesson 4.

    Identify the two nouns joined by の and use the example meaning to understand their relationship.

  5. Session 5: Lesson 5 and review.

    Separate with or and from also or too, finish the checks, and revisit any check that returned after a miss.

One lesson is enough for one sitting. If a lesson remains incomplete, resume it instead of opening another pattern. The app keeps the sequence finite and saves guided grammar progress in this browser.
