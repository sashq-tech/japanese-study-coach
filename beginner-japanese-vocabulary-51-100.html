Japanese Words 51-100

Five more ten-word units for places, time, numbers, descriptions, actions, and questions. The same calm routine continues: study first, check every word, and retry misses.

This block begins after the first 50 words. It expands what you can recognize without pretending that a 100-word foundation is complete N5 vocabulary. Every item below is the exact form used by the interactive course.

Before You Begin

  • Romaji is support. Keep returning to the Japanese form so the Latin spelling does not become the only cue.
  • Say it like is approximate. It helps with a first attempt but cannot show Japanese pitch, vowel length, or the Japanese R sound precisely.
  • Short English meanings are memory cues. Context and grammar can change the best translation.
  • This is not an official JLPT list. It is a practical beginner sequence built from the site's existing kana-first vocabulary seed.

Unit 8: Numbers One Through Ten

Learn one beginner-friendly reading for each number before adding counters and alternate readings.

    Say it like: ee-chee

  2. に - ni

    two
    Say it like: nee

  3. さん - san

    three
    Say it like: sahn

  4. よん - yon

    four
    Say it like: yohn

  5. ご - go

    five
    Say it like: goh

  6. ろく - roku

    six
    Say it like: roh-koo

  7. なな - nana

    seven
    Say it like: nah-nah

  8. はち - hachi

    eight
    Say it like: hah-chee

  9. きゅう - kyuu

Four, seven, and nine also have other common readings. This first pass uses yon, nana, and kyuu; counters and alternate readings belong in a later focused lesson.

Unit 9: Everyday Descriptions

Describe familiar things with a small group of common beginner adjectives.

  1. いい - ii

    good
    Say it like: ee

  2. おおきい - ookii

    big
    Say it like: oh-kee

  3. ちいさい - chiisai

    small
    Say it like: chee-sah-ee

  4. あたらしい - atarashii

    new
    Say it like: ah-tah-rah-shee

  5. ふるい - furui

    old (not a person's age)
    Say it like: foo-roo-ee

  6. たかい - takai

    expensive / tall
    Say it like: tah-kah-ee

  7. やすい - yasui

    cheap
    Say it like: yah-soo-ee

  8. あつい - atsui

    hot
    Say it like: aht-soo-ee

  9. さむい - samui

    cold
    Say it like: sah-moo-ee

  10. おいしい - oishii

    delicious
    Say it like: oh-ee-shee

いい is common in conversation and is related to よい, which appears in some forms. The kana あつい can represent different written words depending on whether the heat is weather, an object, or another context. This course introduces the spoken form before those Kanji distinctions.

Unit 10: More Actions And Questions

Extend simple exchanges with reading, writing, existence, meeting, waiting, and question words.

  1. よむ - yomu

    to read
    Say it like: yoh-moo

  2. かく - kaku

    to write
    Say it like: kah-koo

  3. ある - aru

    to exist / have
    Say it like: ah-roo

  4. いる - iru

    to exist / be there (living things)
    Say it like: ee-roo

  5. あう - au

    to meet
    Say it like: ah-oo

  6. まつ - matsu

    to wait
    Say it like: maht-soo

  7. つかう - tsukau

    to use
    Say it like: tsoo-kah-oo

  8. だれ - dare

    who
    Say it like: dah-reh

  9. いつ - itsu

    when
    Say it like: ee-tsoo

  10. どうして - doushite

    why
    Say it like: doh-shee-teh

Use ある for nonliving things and いる for people and animals in this beginner distinction. Complete sentences still need particles and context, which the grammar path introduces separately.

A Five-Session Routine

  1. Session 1: Places.

    Complete Unit 6 and picture one real errand or destination for each place word.

  2. Session 2: Time.

    Complete Unit 7 and say three true statements using today, tomorrow, or a daily routine word.

  3. Session 3: Numbers.

    Complete Unit 8, then recall the sequence forward and backward without treating this as a counter lesson.

  4. Session 4: Descriptions.

    Complete Unit 9 and pair each adjective with one familiar object or experience.

  5. Session 5: Actions and questions.

    Complete Unit 10, review returned misses, and notice where ある and いる differ.

What Comes After 100 Words

The larger roadmap still targets about 840 beginner words. Reaching 100 means you completed two finite foundations, not that you mastered N5 vocabulary. Continue through the first grammar block, Grammar Lessons 6-10, and the five-scene Hiragana reading practice while this vocabulary remains active.
