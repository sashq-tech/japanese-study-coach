Beginner Japanese Grammar Lessons 6-10

Five short patterns for saying what exists, where something is, asking useful questions, making polite negatives, and requesting a simple item.

These lessons continue the first sentence-builder block. They assume you have met basic topics with は, objects with を, destinations and action places with に／で, noun links with の, and simple uses of と／も. Study the pattern and examples here, then use the guided checks in the app.

Your finish line

    Learn what the pattern does before trying to build or translate a sentence.

  2. Study all three examples.

    Read the Japanese, use the romaji for support, and confirm the plain English meaning.

Lesson 6: Things And People That Are There

Use が with あります for non-living things. Use が with います for people and animals.

みずがあります。

Romaji: Mizu ga arimasu.

Meaning: There is water.

ともだちがいます。

Romaji: Tomodachi ga imasu.

Meaning: There is a friend.

でんわがあります。

Romaji: Denwa ga arimasu.

Meaning: There is a telephone.

Common beginner mistake

Do not use います for an object just because it is important. A telephone and water are non-living, so these examples use あります. A friend is a person, so that example uses います.

Lesson 7: Locations And Positions

Use に with あります／います to identify where something or someone is. Position words connect to a reference noun with の: かばんのなか means inside the bag.

ほんはかばんのなかにあります。

Romaji: Hon wa kaban no naka ni arimasu.

Meaning: The book is inside the bag.

でんわはかばんのうえにあります。

Romaji: Denwa wa kaban no ue ni arimasu.

Meaning: The telephone is on the bag.

ともだちはえきにいます。

Romaji: Tomodachi wa eki ni imasu.

Meaning: The friend is at the station.

Common beginner mistake

Do not automatically use で for every place. Earlier, で marked where an action happened. Here, に marks the location where a thing or person exists.

Lesson 8: Asking Where, What, And Who

Use どこ for where, なに for what, and だれ for who. Keep the question word where its answer would normally appear, then finish the polite question with か.

えきはどこですか。

Romaji: Eki wa doko desu ka.

Meaning: Where is the station?

なにをかいますか。

Romaji: Nani o kaimasu ka.

Meaning: What will you buy?

だれがいますか。

Romaji: Dare ga imasu ka.

Meaning: Who is there?

Common beginner mistake

Do not force English question order onto the Japanese sentence. The particle still shows the question word's job: なにを asks what receives the action, while だれが asks who exists.

Lesson 9: Polite Negative Sentences

Use じゃありません for the polite negative of a noun sentence. In these action examples, the polite verb ends in ません. This lesson does not teach past tense or a complete verb-conjugation system.

これはほんじゃありません。

Romaji: Kore wa hon ja arimasen.

Meaning: This is not a book.

パンをかいません。

Romaji: Pan o kaimasen.

Meaning: I do not buy bread.

にほんごをはなしません。

Romaji: Nihongo o hanashimasen.

Meaning: I do not speak Japanese.

Common beginner mistake

Do not attach じゃありません to every kind of sentence. Learn the complete beginner chunks shown here: a noun plus じゃありません, or the specific polite action form ending in ません.

Lesson 10: Simple Item Requests

Use an item noun with をください to make a simple polite request for that thing.

みずをください。

Romaji: Mizu o kudasai.

Meaning: Water, please.

パンをください。

Romaji: Pan o kudasai.

Meaning: Bread, please.

きっぷをください。

Romaji: Kippu o kudasai.

Meaning: A ticket, please.

Common beginner mistake

This is an item-request pattern, not a rule for turning every verb into a request. Keep the first use narrow: name the thing, add を, and finish with ください.

A Finite Five-Session Routine

  1. Session 1: Lesson 6.

    Sort people from things, explain the difference between あります and います, then clear the lesson checks.

  2. Session 2: Lesson 7.

    Find the reference noun, position word, and location marker before completing the checks.

  3. Session 3: Lesson 8.

    Match どこ／なに／だれ to where, what, and who, then notice the particle after each question word.

  4. Session 4: Lesson 9.

    Separate noun negatives from action negatives and practice only the forms taught in the examples.

  5. Session 5: Lesson 10 and review.

    Practice three item requests, finish the lesson checks, and revisit any check that returned after a miss.

One lesson is enough for one sitting. If a lesson remains incomplete, resume it instead of opening another pattern. The app keeps the sequence finite and saves guided grammar progress in this browser.
