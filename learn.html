Beginner Kana Study Path

A calm route through all 46 basic Hiragana and Katakana, with short recognition lessons, writing practice, and a clear finish line before N5 vocabulary.

I built Japan Ready Coach because I kept buying Japanese books and apps, starting with good intentions, and then drifting away. The problem was not a lack of material. It was a lack of a path I could see and finish. This guide is the front door I wanted: learn one kana row, finish it, continue, and know what comes next.

What Kana Are

Japanese uses two phonetic scripts called Hiragana and Katakana. Each basic character represents a sound rather than an English-style letter. Both scripts cover the same core sounds, but they do different jobs.

  • Hiragana appears in native Japanese words, grammar endings, and particles. It is the first script I would learn.
  • Katakana often appears in foreign names, loanwords, sound effects, and emphasis. It uses the same sound order with different shapes.
  • Kanji are meaning-based characters. They matter later, but they are deliberately outside this first foundation.

There are 46 basic characters in each kana script. Voiced sounds such as ga, za, da, and ba, plus combinations such as kya and sho, build on these shapes. Japan Ready Coach starts with the 46-character base instead of putting every variation on the first screen.

The 22-Lesson Order

The app teaches the 11 basic rows in Hiragana first, then repeats the same sequence in Katakana. Most rows have five characters. The Y row, W row, and final N are shorter because modern basic kana do not fill five slots there.

  1. Vowels: あ い う え お / ア イ ウ エ オ - a, i, u, e, o
  2. K row: か き く け こ / カ キ ク ケ コ - ka, ki, ku, ke, ko
  3. S row: さ し す せ そ / サ シ ス セ ソ - sa, shi, su, se, so
  4. T row: た ち つ て と / タ チ ツ テ ト - ta, chi, tsu, te, to
  5. N row: な に ぬ ね の / ナ ニ ヌ ネ ノ - na, ni, nu, ne, no
  6. H row: は ひ ふ へ ほ / ハ ヒ フ ヘ ホ - ha, hi, fu, he, ho
  7. M row: ま み む め も / マ ミ ム メ モ - ma, mi, mu, me, mo
  8. Y row: や ゆ よ / ヤ ユ ヨ - ya, yu, yo
  9. R row: ら り る れ ろ / ラ リ ル レ ロ - ra, ri, ru, re, ro
  10. W row: わ を / ワ ヲ - wa, wo
  11. Final N: ん / ン - n

Sounds Worth Slowing Down For

Romaji is useful at the beginning, but it is only a reminder. Japanese sounds do not always land exactly where an English speaker expects.

  • し / シ (shi), ち / チ (chi), and つ / ツ (tsu) break the otherwise regular S and T row pattern.
  • ふ / フ (fu) is softer than a typical English f. Treat the romaji as a cue, not a perfect pronunciation spelling.
  • The R row is not a plain English R or L. Early recognition matters first; careful listening and speaking practice should follow.
  • を (wo) keeps its traditional romaji label in charts but is normally pronounced like o when used as the object particle.
  • ん / ン (n) is a standalone nasal sound, not the five-character N row.

How One Guided Lesson Works

  1. Look at one kana character without opening the answer chart.
  2. Choose its matching sound from four choices.
  3. Correct each character in the active row once to finish that lesson.
  4. Use the single Continue button to move to the next row.
  5. Return later through Practice Extras for the stricter three-correct mastery deck, reverse recognition, chart reference, and worksheets.

Completing a row is a first recognition pass, not a claim of mastery. That distinction matters. A visible finish line keeps the session manageable, while the deeper review tools remain available when you are ready to strengthen recall.

A Two-Week Starter Plan

This is the pace I would use if I were rebuilding the habit from zero. It is a suggestion, not a deadline. Repeat a day when the shapes still feel slippery.

  • Days 1-2: Hiragana vowels and K row.
  • Days 3-4: Hiragana S and T rows.
  • Days 5-6: Hiragana N, H, and M rows.
  • Day 7: Hiragana Y, R, W, and final N, followed by a short review.
  • Days 8-13: Repeat the same order in Katakana.
  • Day 14: Use reverse recognition and a blank worksheet to find the characters that still need attention.

Ten focused minutes that you actually complete is more useful than an ambitious hour you avoid. Stop at the end of a row, record the win, and come back for the next one.

Use Writing To Make The Shapes Stick

Writing helped me when I studied Japanese in class, which is why the app includes printable workbook-style practice. Open Step 1 in the app, expand Practice Extras, and choose the printable worksheet.

  • Select Hiragana, Katakana, or both scripts.
  • Print all rows or narrow the sheet to vowels, K/S/T, N/H/M, or Y/R/W plus final N.
  • Use Trace mode to copy a model, then write one from memory.
  • Use Blank Quiz mode when you want prompts plus an answer key.
  • Print on paper or use the browser's Save as PDF option. Nothing is uploaded.

Turn Recognition Into Reading

After the Hiragana rows feel recognizable, practice following a complete thought instead of adding harder characters immediately. The first Hiragana reading routine shows how to decode, group, and check five short scenes already available in the app. It includes a finite 15-minute session and a printable five-scene study log.

Your Stage 1 Readiness Check

You do not need beautiful handwriting or perfect speed before moving forward. I would look for these practical signs:

  • You have completed all 11 guided Hiragana rows and all 11 guided Katakana rows.
  • You can recognize most basic kana without opening the chart.
  • You can work in both directions: kana to sound and sound to kana.
  • You know which small group is still weak and can deliberately review it.
  • You can attempt a blank writing sheet, then use the answer key honestly.

The app saves row progress in your current browser and includes it in the local backup export. There is no account or server sync, so export a backup before clearing browser data or changing devices.

What Comes After Kana

The broader roadmap is intentionally simple: kana first, then practical N5 vocabulary, short grammar examples, and basic kanji. The site already contains starter N5 practice, but it does not yet deliver the full planned sets of roughly 840 vocabulary words, 100 example sentences, or 100 kanji. Those are build targets, not completed-course claims.

The next finite step is the first 50 Japanese words: five ten-word groups covering people, everyday items, travel, actions, and calm exchanges. The guide shows every word and explains the teach-before-check loop before you begin.

Begin guided grammar with the worked guide for Grammar Lessons 1-5: topics, objects, destinations and action places, noun links, and simple uses of と／も. Study each pattern before opening its checks.

After that first block, use the worked guide for Grammar Lessons 6-10 to study existence, locations, question words, polite negatives, and simple item requests before opening their checks.

Japan Ready Coach is also not official JLPT preparation, a substitute for a teacher, or a guarantee that every explanation sounds natural in every setting. The current useful promise is smaller: a beginner can see the first path, complete short lessons, review weak kana, print practice, and know what should come next.
