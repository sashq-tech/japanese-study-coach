Begin after you can recognize most basic Hiragana slowly. Speed is not required. The goal is to move from isolated kana cards to simple hiragana sentence reading: finding who, what, where, and when in a short passage.

Your finish line

Read five passages and answer two comprehension checks for each one. Both answers must be correct in the same attempt to complete a scene. Completion is saved in this browser and included in local backups. This is beginner practice, not an official JLPT reading score or a claim of mastery.

What This Practice Includes

The set is deliberately small: five released hiragana-only passages, ten English questions, optional word clues, and short explanations after each answer. It does not mix in Katakana, Kanji, N4 material, or unreleased reading material.

Why A Short Sentence Feels Harder Than A Kana Card

Japanese normally does not place spaces between every word. A beginner may recognize the kana in わたしはみずをのみます but still lose the meaning while sounding out one character at a time. Particles can also surprise an English reader: the topic particle は is pronounced wa, and the object particle を is commonly pronounced o.

The answer is not to translate every character. Read in small meaning groups, use the grammar markers you already know, and ask one concrete question about the scene.

The Three-Pass Method

  1. Pass 1: Decode without clues.

    Read the whole passage once. Sound out what you can, but do not stop to solve every word. Circle or note only the part that blocks the main idea.

  2. Pass 2: Group the meaning.

    Look for time words, place words, a person or object, and the final action. Open the word clues only after your first attempt, then read the passage again in chunks.

  3. Pass 3: Prove comprehension.

    Answer the two English questions without staring at the choices first. If an answer is wrong, read the explanation, return to the exact clue in the passage, and retry before moving on.

Worked Example: Morning Water

あさです。わたしはみずをのみます。

Meaning groups: あさです。 / わたしは / みずを / のみます。

Romaji support: Asa desu. Watashi wa mizu o nomimasu.

Plain meaning: It is morning. I drink water.

The useful clues are not hidden in a full translation. あさ gives the time, みず names the object, and のみます gives the action. That is enough to answer when the scene happens and what the speaker drinks.

Your Finite Five-Session Plan

  1. Session 1: あさのみず - Morning water

    Find a time word, an object, and an action. Watch how を connects the water to drinking. Finish both checks before stopping.

  2. Session 2: ともだちとえきへ - To the station with a friend

    Identify the destination and companion. The passage uses に for the destination and と for the person going along. Retry if either answer is missed.

  3. Session 3: つくえのうえ - On the desk

    Build the location from smaller parts: desk, above, and book. Then decide what exists and where it is.

  4. Session 4: あしたのがっこう - School tomorrow

    Follow two sentences in order. Separate the destination marker に from the action-place marker で.

  5. Session 5: いえでひるごはん - Lunch at home

    Combine time, place, object, and action. Use で to find where the eating happens, then review any earlier scene that still needs clues.

A 15-Minute Reading Session

  • 2 minutes: Review the kana row that felt slow in your last session.
  • 3 minutes: Read one scene without opening clues.
  • 3 minutes: Open only the clues you need and mark the meaning groups.
  • 5 minutes: Answer both questions, inspect any miss, and retry deliberately.
  • 2 minutes: Read the passage aloud once more, then write one useful word from memory.

One complete scene is a valid session. If you finish early, repeat the passage without clues rather than rushing into a harder script.

Use Clues Without Turning Them Into A Crutch

Word clues are there to keep one unknown word from stopping the whole scene. Try the passage first, reveal the smallest useful clue, and then close the clue panel mentally by reading the complete sentence again. Romaji belongs in the teaching explanation, not over every line of the reading exercise, because the goal is to make the Hiragana carry the sound.

If the same kana shape blocks you several times, return to the row lesson or print a narrow worksheet for that row. If the shapes are readable but the sentence meaning is still unclear, stay with the reading scene and inspect the particles and final verb.

When To Move Forward

Repeat this set until you can read each passage in meaning groups and explain the main idea without relying on the English choices. You do not need perfect speed or perfect handwriting. New reading scenarios stay outside the learner path until they clear the private language-review gate.

If individual characters are still the main obstacle, return to the 22-lesson Kana Study Path. If you can decode short Hiragana lines, go directly to the app's Reading workspace and work through the five scenes in order.
